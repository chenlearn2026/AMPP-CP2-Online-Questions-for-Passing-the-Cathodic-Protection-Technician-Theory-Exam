# AMPP-CP2-Online-Questions-for-Passing-the-Cathodic-Protection-Technician-Theory-Exam
Preparing for the Cathodic Protection Technician theory examination means connecting corrosion principles with measurements, equipment and field records. AMPP-CP2 is the subject name used in this study context; the official theory test code is NACE-CP2-001. The examination assesses the knowledge expected of a minimally qualified CP technician, including intermediate corrosion theory and advanced field measurement concepts.

## Understand the current theory assessment

The November 2025 AMPP Exam Preparation Guide specifies a format change effective December 16, 2025. Preparation should use that updated format rather than the earlier examination length.

- Official test code: NACE-CP2-001.
- Current total time: 3 hours.
- Current length: 110 questions, including 21 additional unscored items.
- Timing includes 4 minutes for the nondisclosure agreement and 6 minutes for the tutorial.
- Item types include multiple choice, some multiple response, and matching.
- Delivery is computer based and closed book, with designated PDF references supplied.
- The current certification-page examination section lists English.
- The course manual is not provided during the examination.

Pass/fail status is provided afterward, together with a domain strengths-and-weaknesses chart. The guide prohibits personal calculators; candidates can use the onscreen calculator and ask the administrator about an available handheld unit.

## Keep the credential separate from the written examination

Cathodic Protection Technician (CP2) certification requires theory and practical examinations, an approved application and the applicable education and experience requirements. Passing theory alone or completing a course does not award the credential.

The current website describes several education/experience pathways and an ethics-training prerequisite. Its treatment of CP1 differs from the November 2025 guide. Confirm your eligibility with AMPP before registration rather than assuming a prerequisite is universally optional or mandatory.

The certification is valid for three years. Its subject matter is relevant to technicians responsible for testing, maintaining and evaluating CP systems, including interference investigation and installation support. It does not replace site authorization or professional supervision.

## Review all eight knowledge areas

Corrosion theory includes electrochemistry, corrosion cells, polarization, Evans diagrams, Nernst and Faraday concepts, electrolyte effects, CP current and reference electrodes. Compare galvanic and impressed-current systems within that foundation.

Equipment and equipment management include meter impedance, calibration, reference-electrode maintenance, ammeters, shunts, digital multimeters and IR-drop measurement errors.

Field measurements include ON/OFF potentials, CIPS, DCVG, ACVG, ACCA, current tests, soil resistivity, casings and interference. Review each method's purpose and limitations.

ICCP power sources cover rectifiers, transformers, conversion, operating modes, protection components, solar supplies, efficiency and diagnostic concepts.

Field data interpretation and documentation include maps, inspection plans, instrument identities, conditions, units, polarity, significant figures, deviations and ECDA-related records.

Field supervision and installation include scheduling support, quality coordination, materials verification, stock management and correct component-installation procedures.

Industry practices and requirements include applicable standards, rules, material-specific protection criteria and accurate, ethical recordkeeping.

Safety covers JSA, permits, PPE, LOTO, electrical hazards, stop-work decisions, step/touch potentials and site-specific precautions. The guide publishes these domains without percentages; do not invent weights.

## Prepare with documented methods and supervised examples

Start by identifying which objectives are unfamiliar. Use AMPP materials and the guide's recommended references to resolve those gaps. Check original diagrams and formula sheets when studying calculations; extracted text can lose mathematical symbols.

Suggested study tasks include comparing reference-electrode concepts, describing survey limitations, reviewing instrument records and explaining what a rectifier schematic represents. Label fictional examples clearly.

Any practical exercise should occur only in an authorized training setting with suitable supervision and safety controls. This article is an examination study framework, not an instruction to work on energized equipment or alter a CP installation.

## Use question review to identify precise errors

Read a question for the requested quantity, units and conditions. For a calculation, write down the principle and unit relationships before selecting an answer. For a scenario, explain why the choice fits the stated requirement.

Compare your answer with another plausible option. If you cannot describe the distinction, return to the relevant source instead of immediately repeating the question.

Group errors by cause: misunderstood concept, incorrect conversion, overlooked measurement condition or misread wording. Keep correct guesses in the review log until you can justify them.

## Finish with a balanced preparation sequence

First build the theory foundation, then connect it with measurement and equipment concepts. Follow with documentation, supervision, industry requirements and safety. End with mixed practice that revisits weaker areas.
